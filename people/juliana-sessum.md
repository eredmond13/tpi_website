Juliana Sessum is a Master's candidate in Cornell University's Data Science for Public Policy program.
Her work explores the application of open-source intelligence and geospatial analysis as tools for
understanding illicit networks and regional security, with past focus on Captagon trafficking in
Syria, conflict risk modeling along the Thailand-Cambodia border, and resistance movements in Myanmar.

She contributed the first documented dataset dedicated to incidents of drone warfare as part of a team
competing in the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency's M.A.J.I.C. Challenge. Her analytic
background includes service as an Intelligence Analyst Intern at United States Central Command, where
she was named The Robertson Foundation for Government's Intern of the Year among 1,483 federal
interns.
