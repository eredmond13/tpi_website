Research

Research Hubs

Our cutting-edge thinking on the critical issues where technology meets national security and public policy.

01

Critical Infrastructure

From secure supply chains to next-generation data centers, the Critical Infrastructure Hub works across physical and digital systems to promote resilience and bolster national security.

The hub is home to the U.S. Semiconductor Research Hub, funded by a $3 million Department of Defense grant. That work centres on improving the resilience of the global interconnected network of semiconductor infrastructure and mitigating future threats, particularly cybersecurity threats. The hub identifies critical vulnerabilities in the semiconductor supply chain, develops targeted mitigation strategies, and informs effective investments to enhance resilience.

  • Semiconductor Supply Chain Security
  • Cyber Test Range
  • Data Centers

02

Drones and Robotics

Catalysed by funding from the NATO Science for Peace and Security Programme, the Drones and Robotics Hub focuses on the development and use of military drone technologies across air, land, and sea.

Current work provides NATO members and partner states with strategic insights on identifying and countering next-generation drone threats, especially lethal autonomous threats, across all domains.

The hub is led by Executive Director Dr. James Patton Rogers and Director Dr. Sarah Kreps, with Senior Fellow Lt. Col. Paul Lushenko, Lead Research Associate Elizabeth H. Redmond, and Director's Fellow Maj. John Miller.

  • Full Spectrum Drone Warfare
  • Counter-drone policy
  • Lethal autonomous systems
  • NATO partnership

Explore Drones and Robotics

03

AI Governance and Defense

Led by Senior Fellow Dr. Chris Earls and Director Dr. Sarah Kreps, and supported by Senior Fellow Judith Germano, this hub investigates how algorithmic systems reshape the conduct and governance of modern defence and security.

The Algorithmic Warfare Research Lab, led by Fellow Joshua Greenberg with Junior Fellows Varija Mehta and Aniket Martins, unites public policy, law, computer science, engineering, philosophy, and sociology to examine how these systems influence human judgment and institutional accountability in conflict.

Work on AI governance, led by Dr. Kreps with Senior Fellow Dr. Doug Kriner and Fellow Adi Rao, addresses frontier questions on AI regulation, the pace of innovation, and misinformation. The hub also runs the Tech Talk speaker series.

  • Algorithmic Warfare Research Lab
  • AI governance and regulation
  • Human judgment and accountability
  • Tech Talk speaker series

Explore AI Governance and Defense

04

Cryptocurrencies

Funded by the Human Rights Foundation and the Reynolds Foundation, the Cryptocurrencies Hub studies how citizens of authoritarian governments secure financial freedom in the face of suppressive financial controls, and investigates how Bitcoin and stablecoins are used around the world.

The goal of this research is to create a global adoption index, mapping cryptocurrency usage in order to advise policymakers, industry leaders, and communities on crafting effective strategies to secure financial freedom.

In 2025 Dr. Kreps debuted the Global Bitcoin Adoption Index at the Oslo Freedom Forum, drawing on 25,000 surveys and 250 interviews across twelve countries. Adoption was driven by financial surveillance, inflation, and the need for banking alternatives in regions with unstable financial systems.

  • Financial freedom under authoritarianism
  • Bitcoin and stablecoin adoption
  • Global adoption index
  • Financial privacy

Explore Cryptocurrencies

05

Geopolitics and Technology

Led by Senior Fellow Dr. Gregory Falco and Executive Director Dr. James Patton Rogers, this hub provides the strategic frameworks used to navigate the global technological competition that defines modern great power rivalry, identifying regions of future strategic importance and securing high-value infrastructure in contested domains.

The Strategic Simulations node uses geostrategic simulation and wargaming to test policy assumptions and reveal emerging risks, working directly with the U.S. Congress, NORAD and NORTHCOM, the U.S. Air Force Academy, NATO partners, and the British Royal Navy.

The Arctic Security node examines strategic competition in the High North and the security of critical infrastructure such as subsea cables, military basing, and ports.

  • Strategic simulations and wargaming
  • Arctic security
  • Subsea cables and critical infrastructure
  • Great power competition

Explore Geopolitics and Technology

06

Space Security

DRAFT. This hub examines the security and governance of space systems at a moment when governments are increasingly dependent on commercial providers for military advantage.

Work spans the cybersecurity of satellite and space systems, the legal and ethical questions raised when commercial assets become legitimate military targets, and the policy vacuum that has opened as private actors become operational gatekeepers in space.

  • Space cybersecurity
  • Commercial space and armed conflict
  • Satellite systems
  • Space governance
