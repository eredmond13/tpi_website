Who we are, what we do

Based within the Jeb E. Brooks School of Public Policy, the Cornell Brooks Tech Policy Institute is the home of technology policy and national security research at Cornell University.

Our multidisciplinary research team works at the highest levels of decision-making, from the White House to the Pentagon, the United Nations to NATO, using academic expertise to advise on critical issues of national and international importance.

Mission

Unlocking potential while mitigating threats

Our mission is to build robust policies that help unlock the potential of AI and Fourth Industrial Revolution technologies, while mitigating emerging threats to security. By partnering with industry, government, and international policy leaders, we leverage our research to solve complex national security and technology policy challenges.

Vision

A growing network of research hubs

Generously funded by a $3 million grant from the U.S. Department of Defense, the institute established the U.S. Semiconductor Research Hub. It was the first of the institute's research hubs, joined by Drones and Robotics, AI Governance and Defense, Cryptocurrencies, and Geopolitics and Technology.

Junior Fellowships

Junior Fellows are recruited from Cornell's highly motivated, academically strong, and talented community of undergraduate scholars. They have shown great initiative, achieved excellence in their academic studies, and apply their skills through research and administrative support within the institute.

Junior Fellows work on live research rather than on the sidelines of it. Recent fellows have spent the summer with the FBI's Cyber Division in New York, joined INTERPOL's Academic Cyber Offender Prevention Network and presented at its headquarters in Lyon, held Laidlaw Scholarships funding international research, and published policy op-eds under their own names on this site.

Fellows are drawn from every school at Cornell, and no single background is expected. What the work rewards is curiosity about how technology and security shape one another, and the persistence to follow a question further than a course would take you.
