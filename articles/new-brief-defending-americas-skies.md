Despite the proliferation of drones across America, policymakers and defence officials have been
hesitant to authorise counter-drone operations in the homeland, fearing backlash over unintended
consequences, especially the loss of American lives and damage to property.

A new brief by Non-Resident Senior Fellow Dr. Paul Lushenko and Dr. Jennifer Spindel of the
University of New Hampshire tests that assumption directly, and finds it largely unfounded. Americans
generally support counter-drone operations in the US homeland.

Support is not unconditional. Three factors shape it, the characteristics of the drone incursion, who
authorises the response, and the effects in terms of Americans' safety.

The study provides the first experimental evidence of what shapes public support for these
operations, with implications for both policy and strategy.
