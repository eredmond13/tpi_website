What happens in the Arctic doesn't stay in the Arctic, especially as climate change, nuclear
deterrence, dual-use technologies, and renewed great power competition converge to reshape the
region's strategic importance.

In response to this growing complexity, members of the institute have been working in partnership
with the Institute of Politics and Global Affairs to develop a national security simulation to help
policymakers confront emerging Arctic challenges.

On July 14th the team took this bipartisan simulation to Capitol Hill and engaged in broader
meetings with Danish and Greenlandic diplomats on Arctic security. While stormy Washington weather
delayed the exercise, its aim was to mirror real-world dilemmas, encouraging members of Congress to
test policies and strategies and build resilience in the face of great power rivalry.

A special thanks to Non-Resident Senior Fellows Paul Lushenko and Keith Carter, and to fellows
Elizabeth Redmond and Kayla Schechter, for the months of work that went into researching and
planning the simulation. Thanks also to Alexandra Gier and the IOPGA team for their organisational
leadership.
