---

Research often assumes that junior military personnel will trust AI during future wars, and trust it
more readily than senior officers do.

New work published in *Armed Forces & Society* by Non-Resident Senior Fellow Dr. Paul Lushenko and
Robert Sparrow tests that claim using an original survey experiment among a representative sample of
cadets in the Reserve Officers' Training Corps programme.

Cadets do turn out to be more trusting of AI-enhanced military technologies than senior officers.
Their trust is shaped, though, by a more conservative understanding of the appropriate use and
oversight of AI than the digital-native framing would predict.

The study offers the first experimental evidence of cadets' trust in AI, with implications for
research, policy, and military modernisation.
