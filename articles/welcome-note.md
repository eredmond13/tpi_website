Welcome! We are launching Tech Policy Signals because we think there is no set of questions more timely, consequential, yet unsettled than those around emerging technologies. Artificial intelligence, semiconductors, drones, digital infrastructure, and other technologies are developing more quickly and have enormous implications for national security, markets, and public policy.

Our aim is to offer independent analysis using data, evidence, and social scientific approaches that can shed light on the opportunities and risks of emerging technologies. We hope that the analysis can be useful to decision makers trying to think through policy choices. That could mean government with respect to whether and what conditions we need new laws or restrictions or whether the existing ones, albeit perhaps with different interpretations, is more appropriate. It can also mean members of industry trying to make sense of the political consequences of what they develop. 

The column will feature contributions from the Director of the Tech Policy Institute at Cornell, Sarah Kreps, who worked on intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance technologies in the Air Force, both with the US and NATO, and then with the directorate of Advanced Systems and Technology in the National Reconnaissance Office. The column will also feature analysis from fellows and researchers affiliated with the Tech Policy Institute. 

The hype around new technologies often makes commentary unmoored from reality. We aim to follow the evidence, examine tradeoffs, and provide analysis over advocacy as we bring clarity to debates that often reduce into simple narratives of promise or peril. We’ll hope you’ll follow along.
