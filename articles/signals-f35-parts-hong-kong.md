summary: "F-35 components were misrouted to Hong Kong and seized by China. Officials call the parts low risk, but the greater warning is about the arms transfer network that carried them."

Since the beginning of warfare, adversarial militaries have often used all means
necessary to gain insight into the weapons of their would-be enemies. For most,
gaining access to this intelligence meant facing great adversity, bypassing
security, and risking great cost and peril. It is exceedingly rare for valuable
military intelligence to be mailed right to an enemy's doorstep. Yet, with little
exaggeration, that is exactly what occurred just this May.

As recent reporting has now revealed, a shipment consisting of components of the
United States' premier F-35 joint strike fighter, [widely considered the world's
most advanced jet fighter thanks to its stealth technology](https://www.af.mil/about-us/fact-sheets/display/article/478441/f-35a-lightning-ii/),
was erroneously shipped to Hong Kong from Australia. [Initially intended to reach
the United States for unconfirmed reasons](https://apnews.com/article/f35-parts-china-hong-kong-australia-c0b248635c79cae9446a2bd7bf4a32f8),
an F-35 canopy and weapons bay door were accidentally routed to Hong Kong, from
where the Chinese Government [seized](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/china-is-holding-sensitive-f-35-parts-diverted-to-hong-kong)
the parts. The disclosure of this incident, given its immense military
implications, has spurred significant [backlash from Congress](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/f-35-parts-diversion-to-hong-kong-under-probe-in-us-congress)
ranging from investigations to statements denouncing the costly mistake as
greatly endangering national security amidst intense geopolitical and military
competition with Beijing.

Officials from the Australian and American governments, as well as Lockheed
Martin, the F-35's manufacturer, have been quick to try to reassure the public by
stating that the parts were not ["sensitive"](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/24/chinaf-f35-components-hong-kong.html)
and were deemed to have a "[low risk of exploitation](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/22/australia/australia-us-f-35-fighter-jet-parts-missing-intl-hnk)".
Yet, [experts warn](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/18/congress-f-35-china-investigation-01083647)
that the stealth coating and materials covering the components, key to the unique
and deadly low radar signature of the aircraft, could enable China to study,
[reverse-engineer](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/18/congress-f-35-china-investigation-01083647),
and even reproduce the technology. If true, that would degrade the effectiveness
of the F-35's stealth properties and therefore reduce its [survivability and
ability to operate in contested airspace](https://www.acc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2888210/airmen-hand-paint-f-35s-for-high-tech-stealth/)
by improving an adversary's ability to detect and engage the aircraft. This would
directly undermine the technological edge of [over 1,200 F-35s fielded by America
and 19 of its allies](https://abcnews.go.com/US/wireStory/pentagon-investigates-after-china-obtains-parts-35-stealth-136704288)
who increasingly rely on the jet to present a credible deterrent to their
adversaries.

Worryingly, this is not the first instance of the F-35's components becoming
unaccounted for. According to a report from the [Government Accountability
Office](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106098), the 1.7 trillion dollar
strike fighter program has seen over a million parts of the jet become "lost".
Yet, considering the [intensive and persistent efforts of China](https://theweek.com/defence/china-f35-parts-hong-kong-us)
to gain access to and intelligence on the program in recent years, this emerges
as potentially the most direct physical insight it has ever gained into one of
the most advanced systems in Washington's arsenal, if not in the entire world.

For the Pentagon, even if it can convince the public that the F-35 isn't now
compromised, it now must convince itself that its logistical supply chains are
secure. The shipment of any military components to an adversarial state
represents not only an unfortunate mistake, but a stark warning that [America's
web of global arms transfers](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/18/congress-f-35-china-investigation-01083647)
opens its own supply chains to greater risks of error and possible manipulation
by adversaries.

For China, access to America's most advanced stealth technology is perhaps
nothing short of a godsend. For Washington, the implications of this incident
have the potential to be disastrous. Yet, the even greater mistake would be for
America to now fail to secure its supply lines, for doing so risks shipping
victory into the hands of the enemy.
